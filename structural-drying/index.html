Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for St. Cloud Restoration know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Our Structural Drying Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. From that map we decide where equipment goes and record our baseline readings.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Monitoring

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.
