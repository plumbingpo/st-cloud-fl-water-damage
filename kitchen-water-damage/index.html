Kitchen Water Damage Help in St. Cloud

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. We help St. Cloud homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Vinyl floor that has discolored or gone soft by the cabinets or the refrigerator
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Refrigerator Water Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Sink Drain Leaks

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.
