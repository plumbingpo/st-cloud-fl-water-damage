Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across St. Cloud, St. Cloud Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
