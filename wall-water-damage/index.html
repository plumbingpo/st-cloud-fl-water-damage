Wall and Drywall Water Damage in St. Cloud

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Wet Sheathing Outside

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Wicking Begins

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.
