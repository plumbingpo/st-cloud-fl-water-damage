Bathroom Water Damage Help in St. Cloud

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help St. Cloud homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Slow Vanity Drips

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.
