Before anything else in a restoration project can work, the water has to be extracted. Structural materials, floors, possessions and building systems all take less damage when standing water comes out sooner. Every hour that water sits on surfaces, it penetrates deeper into materials, soaking through carpet into padding, wicking up drywall, saturating wood framing and seeping into subflooring. Across St. Cloud, St. Cloud Restoration handles emergency extraction with industrial portable and truck-mounted machines that can remove hundreds of gallons a minute.

Few water removal systems match the power of a truck-mounted extractor, which draws water from structural materials, carpeting and hard flooring far better than rental or consumer machines. Where the truck hoses will not reach, we bring in submersible pumps, strong portable extractors and specialty tools made for water caught between floor layers, inside wall cavities or in tight spaces.

Removing visible water is only part of what professional extraction involves. Using moisture detection equipment, our IICRC-certified team finds water that has moved out of sight, whether under cabinets, beneath flooring, behind baseboards or inside walls. Specialty extraction tools remove as much moisture as possible from these hidden areas before the drying phase begins, which significantly reduces the time and equipment needed for structural drying and lowers the risk of secondary mold growth.

Signs It Is Time for Water Extraction

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Moisture appearing along the bottom of walls or at cabinet toe kicks
  • Standing water on any floor surface, whatever the source
  • Carpeting so wet it squelches when you step on it or has puddles on top
  • A utility area, mechanical room or crawl space with water in it
  • Storm water collecting in the lowest level of the home
  • Laminate or wood flooring that feels spongy and pushes up water at the seams
  • Flooded window wells or stairwells letting water into the building

Water Extraction Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Maintain appliance supply lines and replace hoses that show wear or age
  • Test your sump pump before the rainy season so you know it will run
  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is and label it clearly
  • Raise anything you store in the basement a few inches above the floor
  • Look beneath sinks, behind toilets and beside appliances every so often to catch leaks early
  • Leak-sensing shutoff valves can cut off the water on their own when something fails

The Benefits of Professional Water Extraction

Both the harm done to your home and the restoration bill depend directly on quick, thorough extraction.
