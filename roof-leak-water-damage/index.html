Roof Leak Water Damage Help in St. Cloud

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. From indoors, mark where the water enters, and let professionals handle everything else.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Insurance

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. A leak put down to an old, worn or neglected roof is usually denied, and fixing the roof may be treated separately from the interior work. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Attic Insulation

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

Rafters and Decking

Wood in the roof structure that is soaked over and over darkens, swells and softens. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Ceiling Drywall

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. The tape releases along cracked seams, and with enough weight a section can come down suddenly.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.
